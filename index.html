Snowboard Gallery (1989 - 2024)

From the late 1980s to today, snowboard design has evolved from experimental, surf-inspired shapes into highly specialized equipment. Early boards like the Kemper Rampage 160 (1989) and Burton Cruise 165 (1989) reflect long, directional designs built for carving and stability.

By the early 1990s, boards like the Burton Air 5.1 (1992) pushed freestyle progression, while the Burton Air Asym 6 (1994) introduced asymmetrical shaping. Rider-driven models such as the Sims Tina Basich 152 (1994) show the growing influence of athletes on design.

The mid-to-late 1990s saw rapid experimentation. Boards like the K2 Original Fatty (1995) and K2 Dubble Wide (1996) explored width and stability, while the Burton A-Deck series pushed advanced construction. The Burton Custom (1997) became a benchmark for modern all-mountain boards.

In the 2000s, technology matured. Boards like the Ride Timeless (2001) and GNU Carbon High Beam (2001) introduced advanced materials, while freestyle models like the Allian Backman Crayon (2005) reflect park riding’s rise. By 2009, boards such as the Rossignol District and K2 Luna balanced performance with accessibility.

The 2010s introduced rocker and hybrid profiles. Boards like the Never Summer Summit (2010), Burton Feather (2011), Rome SDS Agent (2011), and Bataleon The Jam (2011) show major shifts in board feel and design. Models like the Nidecker Axis (2012) and K2 Sky Lite (2014) expanded all-mountain versatility.

By the 2020s, snowboard design became highly refined. Boards like the Salomon Pulse (2018), Nitro Lectra (2022), and Salomon No Drama (2022) show improved materials and specialization. Modern boards such as the Jones Prodigy (2024) and Salomon Sleepwalker (2024) represent the culmination of decades of innovation.
